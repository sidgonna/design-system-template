          Core · §1

          Filled

          Brand foundation

          A paper ticket you can read in full sun, on a moving boat.

          Brand
          Purpose
          To post the passenger timetable between Gowthami Ghat and the Dowleswaram Barrage steps, so a person at the ghat knows which boat leaves next.
          Promise
          The time on the ticket is the time the boat leaves the pier.
          Audience
          Primary
          People crossing at Rajamahendravaram, often with a bag, a child or a market load, who need a posted time rather than a shouted bargain.
          Secondary
          Visitors who will not take a country boat that has no timetable.
          Positioning
          For people crossing the Godavari at Rajamahendravaram who need a posted time, Godavari Ferry is the passenger boat that leaves when the ticket says it leaves.
          Personality
          plain, punctual, river-literate
          Is not
          a luxury cruise, a startup, a tourist poster
          Competitors to differ from
          unlicensed country boats at the ghat, APTDC pleasure boats, the bus over the Godavari bridge

          Personality sliders (pick a point 1–5):

          Every later section MUST be checkable against these sliders.

          Core · §2

          Filled

          Visual direction and mood

          Ink on warm stone, readable when the sun is on the paper.

          • Mood words: ink, stone, timetable, glare, pier
          • Reference board: {asset_root}/moodboard/ — a paper ticket stub (the object); the Dowleswaram pier rhythm (the mark); a bus-station departure board (the hierarchy); Atkinson Hyperlegible’s x-height (the body); a red-oxide hull number (the secondary).
          • We look like: a printed departure board that got wet and is still readable.
          • We never look like: a cruise brochure, a temple-festival poster, or a software dashboard.
          • Signature move (the one thing people remember): three piers and a waterline, the Dowleswaram barrage reduced to a mark.

A justification is a design decision: record it in design-build/state.yaml → decisions too.

          Core · §6

          Filled

          Voice, tone and copy

          A jetty clerk who has said the same true sentence a thousand times.

          • Voice (constant): plain, punctual, river-literate. Same three words as §1.
          • Tone (shifts by moment):
          MomentToneExample
          WelcomeplainNext boat, pier 2, 06:40.

          The lint reads the Never use for column. The words *backgrounds/fills*, *text/icons* and *borders/outlines* are machine-checked (rule role-slot): for example background: var(--color-text-primary) fails. Other words, like *decoration*, are checked in review.

          • Proportion: About 70% stone surface, 25% night water, 5% brass. Oxide is for a cancelled sailing and the ticket stub, not for decoration.
          • Every fill has its on-colour. brand.primary, brand.secondary, accent and each semantic.* has a color.on.<name>, and each container.<name> a color.on-container.<name>. The lint (on-pair) fails a fill without one, and checks each pair (and each component token pair, component.<c>.<variant>.fg on .bg) at 4.5:1 at rest and under every state layer (§4.3), in every theme and sub-brand.

          Foreground on background, MUST pass §11 contrast in every theme (design.md themes:) and every sub-brand (§13.5). The lint computes each ratio from tokens_file and fails below 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text (at least 18pt, or 14pt bold) and ui (icons, borders, focus rings). Use only these pairings; add a row here before using a new one.

          Interactive states are a layer of the content colour over the fill, at the state.* opacity, never a new colour: background: color-mix(in oklab, var(--component-button-primary-bg), var(--component-button-primary-fg) calc(var(--state-hover) * 100%)). Hover uses state.hover, pressed state.pressed, selected state.selected, a dragged item state.dragged plus shadow.3, and focus adds the color.focus ring (the state.focus layer is for focus on a surface that has no ring). Disabled sets opacity: state.disabled on the whole control (WCAG exempts disabled controls from contrast; the lint skips them). The lint mixes each layer into each fill and re-checks its on-colour (on-pair).

          • Which themes: design.md front matter themes: (first = the default). One theme is fine; then §13 has no modes: block. Any of light, dark, high-contrast, or a named variant (e.g. dim, brand-night).
          • How they switch: the first theme is the default and applies with no attribute. If light or dark is also listed and is not the default, it follows prefers-color-scheme. high-contrast follows prefers-contrast: more. Any theme applies with <html data-theme="name">, which wins over the media query. A theme named dark or *-dark sets color-scheme: dark.
          • Where the values live: §13 modes.<theme> and $extensions.mode in tokens_file. The lint (themes) fails when the front matter and the token modes disagree; every pairing, on-colour and data-series check runs per theme.
          • Gate: WCAG 2 ratios above (4.5 / 3 / 3). APCA Lc is printed next to each pairing (lint info, board pairing table) as advice for the WCAG 3 draft: aim for |Lc| ≥ 75 for body text, ≥ 60 for large text and ≥ 45 for UI, but nothing fails on it.
          • Colour vision: the color.data.* series must stay distinguishable from each other under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia (lint cvd: minimum OKLab ΔE in lint.config.json cvd.min_delta_e, every theme). Colour is never the only signal anyway (§11 color_alone).

          Contrast pairings

          WCAG 2 ratios per theme (the gate) and APCA Lc (advisory, core.md §4.5), computed from the tokens for the default brand; lint.mjs checks every theme, sub-brand and state layer.

          Fluid

          Scale
          font.size.display, font.size.h1, font.size.h2, font.size.h3, font.size.body-lg, font.size.body, font.size.caption, font.size.overline

          • Type roles, not loose sizes. Each type.* role is one DTCG typography token (family, size, weight, line height, letter spacing, plus OpenType features) and becomes --type-<role> for the CSS font shorthand, with --type-<role>-font-size and the other parts beside it: h1 { font: var(--type-h1); letter-spacing: var(--type-h1-letter-spacing); }. Inside :lang(te) and :lang(hi) each role switches to the §12 family and font.line-height.script. The roles map one to one onto the Google DESIGN.md typography schema.

          • MUST NOT use more than 2 families in one piece.
          • Loading and fallback: Fraunces (heading) and Atkinson Hyperlegible (body) ship as SIL OFL woff2 files in assets/fonts/, declared with @font-face and font-display: swap in assets/fonts/fonts.css. Source Serif 4 is the second family in the heading stack and is shipped too, so a missing Fraunces glyph does not fall through to a system serif. JetBrains Mono, Noto Sans Telugu and Noto Sans Devanagari ship the same way.

          Slow

          "Everyone gets the full experience."

          A11y
          Standard
          WCAG 2.2 AA
          Contrast
          { body_text: 4.5:1, large_text: 3:1, ui_and_icons: 3:1 }

          Breakpoints and responsive rules

          Uses breakpoint.*. At phone width the next boat is one column. At tablet the day list sits beside the pier name. At desktop the pier board and the booking column share the page. Type does not shrink below type.body.

          • Every layout MUST work at the three check widths viewport.mobile, viewport.tablet and viewport.desktop, in light and dark: no horizontal scroll, no key element off-screen, no overlapping controls. design-build/render.mjs screenshots and checks each one.
          • The two scales do different jobs. breakpoint.* (sm 640, md 768, lg 1024, xl 1280 px) are where the layout changes; viewport.* (390, 768, 1280 px) are the device widths the checks use, so mobile is a phone below sm, tablet sits on md and desktop on xl. Check any extra width the layout changes at with render.mjs --widths.
          • CSS variables do not work inside @media or @container, so write the breakpoint's px number there (for md, min-width with the value of breakpoint.md in px). The lint accepts a media-query width only when it equals a breakpoint.* or viewport.* token. Use var(--breakpoint-md) only in JS (matchMedia) or in max-inline-size for content widths.

            Data visualization

            Series colors color.data.*, axis/grid style, label rules, chart types allowed, never use 3D or pie with more than 4 slices. NARROWS §4.5: series are assigned in order color.data.1, color.data.2, …; the set stays distinguishable under protanopia, deuteranopia and tritanopia (lint cvd), and every series also gets a direct label, marker shape or dash pattern so colour is never the only signal (§11).
